Estimation of Chloride Content of a Water Sample

Mohr’s Method – Argentometric Titration

Aim

To determine the amount of chloride present in 100 mL of the given water sample using standard sodium chloride solution and approximately N/100 silver nitrate solution.

Principle

Water generally contains chloride ions (Cl⁻) in the form of sodium chloride (NaCl), potassium chloride (KCl), calcium chloride (CaCl₂) and magnesium chloride (MgCl₂). The total chloride content can be determined by the argentometric method, also called Mohr’s method.

In this method, the chloride ion solution is directly titrated against silver nitrate (AgNO₃), using potassium chromate (K₂CrO₄) as an indicator. Silver ions react with chloride ions to form a white precipitate of silver chloride.

AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃
White precipitate of AgCl

When all chloride ions have been precipitated, the first slight excess of silver ions reacts with chromate ions from the indicator. The appearance of a persistent reddish-brown precipitate of silver chromate indicates the endpoint.

2AgNO₃ + K₂CrO₄ → Ag₂CrO₄↓ + 2KNO₃
Yellow indicator → reddish-brown endpoint
Endpoint: Change from yellow to a persistent reddish-brown colour.

Materials required

Standard NaCl solution, AgNO₃ solution, 2% K₂CrO₄ indicator, burette, pipette and conical flask.

Calculation principle

V₁N₁ = V₂N₂    therefore    N₁ = (V₂ × N₂) / V₁

Amount of chloride (g/L) = 35.46 × N of chloride solution

Chloride content (mg/L or ppm) = 35,460 × N of chloride solution

Before starting: Read the principle, reactions, endpoint and calculation. In this simulation, the solution-preparation quantities are provided as calculated teaching values. Follow your laboratory instructor’s directions when preparing real reagents.

Step 1 – Solution Preparation

Prepare or identify the following solutions before beginning the titrations. The supplied laboratory procedure specifies standard NaCl of 0.01 N, approximately N/100 AgNO₃ and 2% K₂CrO₄ indicator.

A. Standard sodium chloride solution (editable concentration)

Equivalent weight of NaCl = 58.44 g/eq.

Mass (g) = N × equivalent weight × volume (L)

Mass required for 100 mL: 0.05844 g NaCl.

Dissolve the calculated mass in distilled water and make up to 100 mL in a volumetric flask. Change the normality to assign a different standard solution to each student.

B. Silver nitrate solution (editable approximate concentration)

Equivalent weight of AgNO₃ = 169.87 g/eq.

Mass required for 100 mL: 0.16987 g AgNO₃.

Dissolve in distilled water and make up to 100 mL. This is the approximate concentration used to simulate the burette delivery; its exact normality is determined by standardization in Step 2.

C. Potassium chromate indicator (2% w/v)

For a 2% w/v solution, dissolve 2.0 g K₂CrO₄ in distilled water and make up to 100 mL.

Safety: Silver nitrate can stain and irritate skin and eyes. Potassium chromate contains hexavalent chromium and is hazardous. Wear gloves, goggles and a lab coat; handle and dispose of solutions as instructed by your laboratory.

Titration I – Standardization of AgNO₃

Titration: 20.0 mL student-prepared standard NaCl (selected normality) versus approximate AgNO₃ in the burette.

  1. Wash the burette with distilled water and rinse it with a small quantity of AgNO₃ solution.
  2. Fill the burette with AgNO₃ solution to the zero mark, ensuring that no air bubbles remain.
  3. Rinse the pipette with standard NaCl solution and transfer 20.0 mL into a clean conical flask.
  4. Add 1.0 mL of 2% K₂CrO₄ indicator. The solution appears yellow.
  5. Titrate with AgNO₃ while mixing until a persistent reddish-brown colour appears. Repeat to obtain concordant readings.
Standardization relation: V(NaCl) × N(NaCl) = V(AgNO₃) × N(AgNO₃).

Fixed NaCl volume: 20.00 mL
NaCl normality: 0.0100 N
AgNO₃ prepared concentration: 0.0100 N (approx.)
Indicator: 1.0 mL K₂CrO₄

Titration II – Estimation of Chloride in Water

Use the standardized AgNO₃ solution obtained in Titration I.

  1. Rinse the pipette with the given water sample and transfer 20.0 mL into a clean conical flask.
  2. Add 1.0 mL of 2% K₂CrO₄ indicator.
  3. Titrate against standardized AgNO₃ in the burette until the yellow colour changes to persistent reddish-brown.
  4. Repeat the titration to obtain concordant readings.
Use a different assigned value for each student to obtain different final chloride results.
